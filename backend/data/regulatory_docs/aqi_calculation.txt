CPCB National Air Quality Index (AQI) Calculation Methodology
Publication Date: October 2014

The National Air Quality Index (AQI) in India is a tool to communicate air quality status to people in terms that are easy to understand. It transforms complex air quality data of various pollutants into a single number.

1. AQI Categories and Breakpoints:
   - Good (0 - 50): Minimal impact.
   - Satisfactory (51 - 100): Minor breathing discomfort to sensitive people.
   - Moderate (101 - 200): Breathing discomfort to people with lungs, asthma, and heart diseases.
   - Poor (201 - 300): Breathing discomfort to most people on prolonged exposure.
   - Very Poor (301 - 400): Respiratory illness on prolonged exposure.
   - Severe (401 - 500): Affects healthy people and seriously impacts those with existing diseases.

2. Calculation Formula:
   The AQI is calculated based on sub-index values of individual pollutants. The sub-index (Ii) for a given pollutant concentration (Cp) is calculated using linear interpolation:
   
   Ii = [ ((Ihi - Ilo) / (Bphi - Bplo)) * (Cp - Bplo) ] + Ilo
   
   Where:
   - Bphi = Breakpoint concentration greater than or equal to Cp
   - Bplo = Breakpoint concentration less than or equal to Cp
   - Ihi = Sub-index value corresponding to Bphi
   - Ilo = Sub-index value corresponding to Bplo
   - Cp = Pollutant concentration

3. Calculating Overall AQI:
   - The overall AQI is the maximum of the individual sub-indices: AQI = max(I1, I2, I3, ..., In)
   - To calculate AQI, sub-indices for at least three pollutants must be calculated, out of which one must be either PM2.5 or PM10.
   - The dominant pollutant (the one with the maximum sub-index) is reported as the primary pollutant.
