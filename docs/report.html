1. Problem Analysis

India's air quality crisis is a national, not regional, problem. The Lancet estimates 1.67 million premature deaths annually from air pollution in India; Dalberg/CII put the economic cost at ~$95B per year — roughly 3% of GDP. In 2024-25, Delhi averaged an AQI of 218 ("Poor" or worse for 200+ days); Mumbai recorded dangerous AQI on 60+ days; Bengaluru and Chennai — historically cleaner metros — are measurably deteriorating as vehicle density and construction activity surge.

The gap is not data — India has deployed 900+ CAAQMS stations under NCAP, and the official real-time feed is published on data.gov.in. The gap is execution between the reading and the response:

The Key Systemic Failures:
  1. The government's own graded-response policy is executed reactively. CAQM's Graded Response Action Plan (GRAP) states its stages "shall be invoked in advance" based on forecasts. Yet 13 of 17 GRAP invocations in winter 2025-26 were reactive (ThePrint/CEEW). The concrete failure: on 10 Nov 2025 Delhi hit AQI 362; GRAP Stage III came only on 11 Nov, at 425+ — more than 24 hours after the signal, at the cost of a full day of statutory protections.
  2. No source attribution. Administrators see "AQI 320" but not why — traffic, an industrial stack, construction dust, or crop burning upwind? Without that, enforcement is a guess. Only Delhi buys real-time apportionment today (DPCC pays IIT-Kanpur crores for it); the other cities get nothing.
  3. No actionable forecasting. Only 8 of the 131 NCAP non-attainment cities have any early-warning system. Response is keyed to what a sensor reads now, not to what conditions will be in 24-72 hours, when interventions (truck bans, construction halts, school closures) could still be scheduled to matter.
  4. No correlation across signals. A single elevated PM2.5 reading looks the same whether it is a transient blip or the leading edge of a genuine compound event (breach + worsening forecast + a dominant, matching source). No tool in common use makes that distinction automatically.

The sharpest framing of the opportunity: India already wrote the policy that responds to forecasts. It fails to execute it on time. That is an automation problem — and automation is what this platform does.

2. Solution Overview — One Chain, One Win

VayuDrishti is a full-stack platform (FastAPI + React/Leaflet + MongoDB + XGBoost + LangChain/Gemini) built around a single spine — every feature is a link in one chain, which is also the demo's war-room flow:

The War Room Operational Chain
1
Forecast sees the crisis coming: 24–72h multi-horizon XGBoost forecaster beats persistence at every horizon.
2
GRAP Automation drafts statutory order: Drafts Stage III/IV orders BEFORE threshold crossing (24-48h lead time).
3
Evidence attached: CPF wind-sector source attribution + 570km HYSPLIT-lite back-trajectories over active Punjab crop fires.
4
Human impact quantified: People exposed, AQLI life-years lost, WHO AirQ+ daily excess deaths averted.
5
Citizens alerted in regional languages: Multi-lingual TTS voice advisories (Hindi, Tamil, Bengali, Telugu, Kannada).
✓
Result: Signal → drafted statutory intervention in seconds, vs the documented >24h government lag.

Everything runs with zero API keys (physically-grounded simulator + deterministic rule fallbacks + in-memory database fallback), and every panel carries a provenance badge (LIVE HISTORICAL REPLAY MODELLED) so nothing is faked.

3. The Ten Shipped Capabilities

3.1 Forecast-Triggered GRAP Automation Engine backend/services/grap.py

A rules layer over the forecaster using official CAQM stage thresholds (Stage I 201-300 · II 301-400 · III 401-450 · IV >450). The trigger is the earliest sustained (≥3h) threshold crossing detected by any of three transparent signals — observed, XGBoost forecast, or persistence-with-trend projection. Auto-generates CAQM-style statutory orders with real action checklists (BS-III petrol/BS-IV diesel ban, C&D halt, truck bans, online classes) tagged to responsible agencies. Endpoints: GET /api/v1/grap/status, /grap/schedule.

3.2 Direct Multi-Horizon Forecaster + TreeSHAP backend/services/prediction.py

One XGBoost model per horizon (6/12/24/48/72h), each predicting directly and interpolated into the hourly curve. Evaluated on a chronological 14-day holdout against naive persistence ("AQI in Nh = AQI now"):

HorizonModel RMSEPersistence RMSEImprovement
24 Hours13.8319.00+27.2%
48 Hours13.7619.22+28.4%
72 Hours13.7818.78+26.6%

Every forecast ships an exact TreeSHAP explanation via XGBoost's native pred_contribs (no external dependency): "forecast +38 AQI: 24h-ago AQI +14, low wind +9, current PM2.5 +11."

3.3 CPF Source Attribution with Real Confidence backend/services/attribution.py

CPF (Conditional Probability Function) wind-sector probabilities — the peer-reviewed openair receptor-modelling diagnostic — as statistical backbone, rendered as a polar pollution rose. Priors calibrated to published Delhi PMF source-apportionment splits; likelihoods from measured station chemistry. Live covariates are NASA FIRMS + TomTom; every covariate carries a source label (live: / measured: / catalog: / modelled:) with real confidence fields. Zero random inputs.

3.4 HYSPLIT-lite Back-Trajectories × Fire Fusion backend/services/trajectory.py

A 2D kinematic back-trajectory integrating air parcels backward hour-by-hour through transport wind, intersecting corridors with fire detections: "air mass crossed 10 active Punjab/Haryana fires within 17 hours — 570 km traced" — animated on the map. Endpoint: GET /api/v1/trajectory/back.

3.5 Health & Exposure Impact Engine backend/services/health_impact.py

Three cited lenses: AQLI life-years lost (EPIC/UChicago, 0.098 life-years per µg/m³ PM2.5, annual-equivalent capped at 130 µg/m³), WHO AirQ+ short-term mortality (RR 1.08 per 10 µg/m³ PM2.5, capped at 150 µg/m³), and population-weighted exposure (~4.78M people covered across Delhi stations). Converts concentrations to decision language: "protects 4.78M people, averts ~13 deaths/day."

3.6 Dec 2025 Crisis Replay backend/services/replay.py

A one-click, honestly-labelled HISTORICAL REPLAY of the 13-16 Dec 2025 Delhi Severe+ episode (Anand Vihar 644, Wazirpur 635, Mundka 560, Mandir Marg 519) wired through every service via an at timestamp parameter. Replay runs are ephemeral and pytest-enforced never to pollute live databases.

3.7 Incident War Room frontend/src/pages/WarRoom.jsx

One orchestrated view running the full 6-step pipeline for worst stations with a real wall-clock "signal → drafted intervention" counter displayed beside the documented government benchmark (>24h lag).

3.8 Voice / IVR Citizen Channel frontend/src/hooks/useSpeech.js

Browser-native TTS in hi-IN / ta-IN / bn-IN / te-IN / kn-IN — zero backend, zero quota — plus a phone-frame IVR preview and WhatsApp mock. CPCB's SAMEER app is English-only; VayuDrishti speaks 6 regional languages.

3.9 Official data.gov.in CPCB Feed Adapter backend/services/gov_feed.py

Live adapter for the GoI CAAQMS resource on data.gov.in — verified live: 3,500 records reachable, 500+ CAAQMS stations published nationwide. Onboarding a city is a config entry, not an integration project.

3.10 Tool-Calling Agents + Compliance RAG backend/services/agents.py

Two genuine LangChain bind_tools() agents (Compound-Risk Enforcement Agent & Citizen Advisory Agent) querying ChromaDB RAG over NAAQS/NCAP/AQI standards with TF-IDF fallback and Mongo-backed LLM cache.

3.11 Provenance Badges Everywhere frontend/src/components/common/ProvenanceBadge.jsx

Every data panel carries explicit provenance tags (LIVE HISTORICAL REPLAY MODELLED) ensuring zero fake metrics.

4. Technical Foundations

  • Data Layer (backend/services/data_ingestion.py): Live AQI from AQICN (with OpenWeatherMap fallback) and Open-Meteo weather (keyless), Indian NAQI computed from CPCB sub-index tables, falling back to a physically-grounded simulator.
  • Scheduler (backend/services/scheduler.py): APScheduler hourly live ingestion + 65-min enforcement anomaly scans.
  • Database Fallback (backend/models/database.py): MongoDB via Motor with transparent in-memory MockCollection fallback ($in, $gte, $lte, sorting, indexing).
  • Automated Testing: 57 pytest tests green covering calculations, attribution guards, predictions, replay ephemerality, and serialization.

8. Scalability Analysis

  • Adding a city is a stations.json entry plus coordinates — no router/service code changes. Demonstrably speaks GoI data.gov.in format covering 500+ stations.
  • Pooled ML Model is station-agnostic; onboarding does not require retraining per station.
  • MongoDB Indexed on (station_id, timestamp) and city.
  • Agent cost scales with usage, not station count — cached-first LLM layer means repeated queries cost zero quota.
